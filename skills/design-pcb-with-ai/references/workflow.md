# PCB design workflow

## Brief and capability gate

Inspect the actual tool connection and existing EDA project before assuming access. Main AI prepares `pcb-brief.json` with `version:1`, `revision`, `critical_unknowns`, `software:{name,version,connection_usable}`, `agents_available`, `supply:{voltage_v,source,current_limit_ma}`, `outline:{shape,width,height,units}`, `parts:[{ref,model,quantity,pins,footprint,footprint_source}]`, `nets:[{name,endpoints,source}]`, `connectors`, `holes`, `keepouts`, `fabrication:{route,process,constraints}`, `model_gaps` and `checks`. Use actual pin names (`U1:VCC`), exact part revisions and official drawing/datasheet sources. Complex outlines need their exact contour file and units as well as dimensions. Identify connector orientation/access, hole size/location, keepout dimensions, copper/clearance/drill/stackup and process limits. A model alone does not establish pad mapping.

The fictional example deliberately has unknown footprints and no EDA connection; it is a draft. Do not guess those fields or call the example manufacture-ready. Run `python scripts/pcb_brief.py PATH` for an offline summary. `ready-for-eda` means the checked brief is ready to hand to a child; it is NEVER a finished schematic/PCB. The helper cannot inspect EDA, establish engineering truth or grant rights. Capability flags must come from real current tool checks. When agents or software access are missing, leave a useful draft with one concrete blocker and existing files; no forced install. The two product entries route here only on a PCB request.

Each net name is unique and each physical endpoint belongs to exactly one listed net. Duplicate/conflicting names, repeated endpoints or a pin on two nets leave the brief draft. Ordinary separate one-pin nets and multi-endpoint nets remain supported; this identity check does not simulate the circuit.

## Design and actual evidence

The persistent PCB child uses the existing usable EDA project, preserves source, checks schematic/netlist/ERC, maps each actual part pin to its exact footprint pad and polarity, then places/routes against outline/connectors/holes/keepouts/process constraints. The firmware child owns firmware separately. Max two concurrent children and one writer per scope; serialise shared project changes. Return compact evidence rather than source dumps. A missing connection does not justify bypassing software security or claiming finished work.

After DRC, inspect virtual assembly with available exact part models: dimensions, orientation, body/lead clearance and connector access. Model gaps are explicit unknowns and restrict claims; do not infer a checked fit from a generic box. Use circuit simulation selectively for a relevant uncertainty when trusted models exist; record model assumptions and coverage. No project-wide mandatory simulator or physical test. Report electrical review, ERC, footprint/pad/polarity review, DRC/routing, virtual assembly, circuit simulation and hardware observations separately with actual files/screenshots/log summaries and revision.

`checks` entries use `{kind,status,revision,path,sha256}`. Supported kinds are `erc`, `footprint`, `drc`, `assembly`, `simulation`, `export-reopen`. `passed`, `failed` or `limited` needs a real existing report, matching revision and SHA256; missing evidence becomes unknown. `not-run`/`not-applicable` are explicit. Reports must describe tool/version, checked sources, observations and limits; a supplied hash does not prove the report's truth. The helper only reads relative allowlisted reports and has no EDA/process/network/device APIs. Changed source must invalidate/regenerate affected reports, not merely rename the revision. Main AI evaluates whether evidence proves the claim.

## Output by process

Keep editable native EDA sources, schematic PDF/image, PCB/layout, exact BOM, netlist/connections, check reports and model/verification limits. For a fabricator include final Gerber copper/mask/silkscreen/outline and drill outputs; include placement/assembly/BOM data if assembly is requested. For DIY include the exact process's 1:1 copper transfer/exposure, outline and drill guides with dimensions/units/scale and mirror convention. No generic G-code: exact machine, tool, stock, work origin, units, process and safety limits must be known before any toolpath is offered. Do not operate fabrication tools.

Reopen the final exported files in an available viewer/importer, check layers, units, scale, holes, outline and source-revision consistency, then record their digests. Merely exporting or viewing the EDA source is not an exported-file readback. Critical unresolved part/net/power/footprint/process facts leave the design draft. A noncritical missing model may permit documented manufacturing progress after a real fit review, but never a full virtual-clearance pass. Final reproduction also needs all project-specific firmware/build/assets/tools/setup and assembly/use/acceptance instructions, rights and exact versions; no hidden files or future AI redesign. Do not promise electrical safety, zero errors or identical physical tolerances from virtual checks.
